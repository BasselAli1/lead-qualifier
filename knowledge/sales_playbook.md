# Sales Playbook

## Strong buying signals

Notes that mention a specific trigger event (new funding round, recent
leadership hire, a compliance deadline, an expiring contract with a
competitor) indicate active, time-boxed intent rather than casual
browsing. A stated timeline ("need this live by Q3") or an explicit
comparison against named competitors are both strong positive signals —
the lead is already evaluating, not just researching.

## Weak or neutral signals

Generic requests ("interested in learning more", "send me pricing") with
no context about company size, team, or problem being solved are weak on
their own. Don't penalize these leads outright — the CRM data (company
size, industry, title) may still make them a good fit even if the free
text notes are thin — but don't inflate the score based on enthusiasm of
tone alone.

## Disqualifying signals

Notes indicating the lead is a student, is doing academic research, is a
competitor doing research, or is explicitly job-hunting/recruiting
(rather than evaluating the product) should score very low regardless of
firmographic fit.

## Past deal grounding

When past-deal notes are present in the retrieved context, treat them as
evidence of what a real closed-won or closed-lost deal actually looked
like at this stage — use them to calibrate how confident to be, not as a
guarantee that a similar-sounding lead will close the same way.
