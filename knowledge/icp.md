# Ideal Customer Profile

## Firmographics

We sell best into mid-market and enterprise software/SaaS/technology
companies. Fewer than 5 employees is an auto-disqualify (not enough
budget authority or process to close). 200+ employees is a strong
positive signal — these companies have dedicated budget for tooling in
our category and a real evaluation process.

## Industry fit

Best fit: Software, SaaS, Technology. We have supporting case studies and
proven ROI in these verticals. Companies outside these industries aren't
disqualified, but they should be treated as an unproven fit unless the
lead's notes describe a specific, well-understood use case.

## Buyer persona

The ideal contact holds real budget authority: VP, Director, Head of,
Chief-level (C-level), or Founder. A lead from an individual contributor
or junior title can still be worth engaging, but should be scored lower
on intent unless they explicitly mention they're evaluating on behalf of
a decision-maker.

## Budget

A stated budget of $10,000 or more meets our minimum deal size. Below
that, the deal is likely too small to be worth our sales cycle unless
there's a clear path to expansion (e.g. a pilot for a much larger team).

## Served geography

We only actively sell into the US, Canada, UK, and Australia — we don't
have the legal/support infrastructure to serve other geographies yet.
Leads outside these countries are auto-disqualified before reaching the
LLM.

## Red flags

Vague or empty notes with no stated problem, budget, or timeline are a
weak signal on their own — score conservatively rather than assuming
intent that isn't actually there in the lead's notes.
